Bridging the Gap Between Engineering and Medicine: Rehabilitation of Stroke, Parkinson and Other Neurologic Disorders

Did you know that 7% of US adults cannot walk 1/4 mile? Clinicians increasingly rely on engineers for developing rehabilitation techniques for neurologic disorders affecting movement. This course focuses on the engineering research underlying the development of clinical therapy interventions. You will have hands-on experience with equipment and protocols used for research and patient treatment during multiple visits to the Kennedy Krieger Institute – a world-renowned institution that studies and treats disorders of the nervous system. The course involves a group project component where students will design, carry out, and analyze data from their own experiment that uses a split-belt treadmill. For remote sessions: You will have hands-on experience with designing research protocols aimed at improving pandemic-time telehealth: you will create your own neurorehabilitation game in Unity based on cutting-edge neuroscience principles.

Engineering Rehabilitation or Motor, Neurologic and Psychiatric Disorders

This 10-week, 1-credit course dives into neurorehabilitation research, as part of the Hopkins Engineering Applications & Research Tutorials program targeted at freshmen. The course provides an overview of the engineering research into modern neurorehabilitation techniques for patients with motor, cognitive and psychiatric disorders. It will emphasize the role of engineers in the development of devices and in the investigation of therapy treatments that will be used in hospitals and clinics. Students will learn the material through lectures, interaction with patients and hands-on laboratory activities. The course involves a group project component where students will design, carry out, and analyze data from their own experiment that uses a split-belt treadmill. For remote sessions: In light of the pandemic, the course will include techniques for tele-rehabilitation. Students will design & implement their own tele-rehabilitation game in Unity, and they will test it and analyze data from classmates. Interaction with patients may be cancelled.

Biomedical Engineering: Rehabilitation and Devices

The course explores the biomedical research and tools used for rehabilitation and medical devices. In the first week, we will discuss rehabilitation of movement and psychiatric disorders, and the research into rehabilitation. In the second week, we will talk about devices such as neural prosthesis and smartphone aids for the visually impaired. Students will have first-hand experience with equipment during lab visits, and will interactively learn to design research questions and analyze data. With your group, you will collect and analyze your own kinematic data on a treadmill used for stroke rehabilitation research, and prepare a scientific paper on your findings using neuroscience and neurology principles. You will also design and analyze your own prosthetic device in COMSOL using mechanical and material engineering principles.

Miracles of Modern Medicine: Neuroengineering, Motor & Stroke

This one-day course will offer an overview into the form of motor learning called “motor adaptation”, and its impact on modern neurorehabilitation techniques. Movement in humans is extremely complex, and surprisingly extremely flexible. However, damage to the brain can severely impact movement and its flexibility. Movement rehabilitation is extremely important after neurologic injury like stroke, but a lot is still unknown about the different neural mechanisms for movement. How are scientists tackling this problem? What does the future of neurorehabilitation look like? In this course, we will explore the role of different brain regions in human motor control. We will discuss what we already know as well as the type of research that is currently being done to understand what we still do not know. Through hands-on exposure to research equipment, we will explore how scientists are trying to take advantage of the neural circuits which are still intact after neurologic injury to develop better rehabilitation treatments.

Statistical Methods for data Analysis

Useful methodological resources, mostly written by other individuals. Highly recommended as resources to support robust research methodologies.

Bootstrapping & Confidence Intervals
  • Efron, B., & Tibshirani, R.J. An Introduction to the Bootstrap. Chapman and Hall/CRC. 1994.Great for learning bootstrapping - how to implement it in code, how to choose the appropriate implementation for your research question & data, and how to interpret your results statistically.
Linear & Non-linear models, Random & Mixed Effects
  • Mixed Models – Repeated Measures. NCSS Statistical Software , Chapter 222.Intuitive introduction to repeated-measures designs and mixed models.
  • Nathaniel E. Helwig. Linear Mixed-Effects Regression. Slides, 2017.Intuitive introduction to repeated-measures designs, ANOVA & Linear Mixed-Effects Regression.
  • Weighted least squaresMcMaster University › teaching › Lectures 7.How to Implement Weighted Least Squares regression.
  • Venables, Bill & Ripley, B. Modern Applied Statistics With S. Springer 2002.Great introduction to Random & Mixed Effects Models, including both linear, generalized linear and non-linear models. It also include explanations of confidence intervals as well as a section on how to perform bootstrapping on linear models. (Note that much of the S coding can be easily implemented in R).
  • James, Gareth, Daniela Witten, Trevor Hastie, and Robert Tibshirani. An Introduction to Statistical Learning. PDF. 1st ed. Springer Texts in Statistics. 2013.Great explanations on linear & non-linear models - in particual, inclduing logistic models and logistic regression. It also includes principal component analysis & support vector machines. Sections on smoothing methods like spline.
  • Cosma Rohilla Shalizi. Advanced Data Analysis from an Elementary Point of View. Preprint of book found at https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~cshalizi/ADAfaEPoV/. Great explanations on regression & bootstrapping. Particularly recommend Chapter on Logistic Regression.
  • Zhang, Biao. A Chi-Squared Goodness-of-Fit Test for Logistic Regression Models Based on Case- Control Data Biometrika , Sep., 1999, Vol. 86, No. 3, pp. 531-539. 1999. For learning when & how to implement Chi-Squared test in logistic regression.
Stats & Maths Foundations
  • Leow Wee Kheng. Matrix Differentiation. Class Slides from CS4243 Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition
  • Yen-Chi Chen. Model Selection and Prediction. Lecture 7 in STAT 535: Statistical Machine Learning. Autumn 2019.Great explanation of AIC (Akaike information criterion) and BIC (Bayesian information criterion).
Neuroscience & Neurorehabilitation

The resources provided here are mostly books or articles written by other individuals. They cover topics ranging from basic neuroscience concepts to current research & technologies to principles of medical & physical therapy practice. I recommend these readings to anyone who wants to deepen their understanding of course material and/or explore academic/career directions!

Neuroscience Concepts
  • Kandel, Eric R., et al., editors. Principles of Neural Science. 5th ed., McGraw Hill Medical, 2013Gold standard, primary textbook for neuroscience education. It is a comprehensive resource explaining established knowledge about our nervous system and how it links to behavior. (Note that the link is for the 5th edition - not the latest edition)
  • Steven Bassett. The Nervous System: Sensory and Motor Tracts of the Spinal Cord. Lecture Presentations, Southeast Community College. Lincoln, Nebraska. Pearson Education, Inc. 2012.Great intuitive introduction to neuroscience of the sensorimotor system.
  • Neuroscience Basics - Memory Game (by Cris Rossi)
  • Virtual NeuronsNeuron firing game/simulation.
  • Brain Explorer at BrainFacts.orgInteractive exploration of different brain regions.
Research & Technology
  • Gescheider, George A. The Classical Psychophysical Methods. In Psychophysics: The Fundamentals. Chapter 3. Lawrence Erlbaum Associates, 1997.Great introduction to psychophysical methods to test perception in the lab in human participants.
  • Reinkensmeyer, David J., and Volker Dietz, eds. Neurorehabilitation Technology. Second edition. Switzerland: Springer, 2016. Print. Comprehensive overview of current technological interventions to improve human movement after neurological injuries such as stroke & spinal cord injury. Covers technologies including brain stimulation, VR, robotic devices, brain-computer interfaces.
  • Carter, Matt, and Jennifer C. Shieh. Guide to Research Techniques in Neuroscience. Academic Press, 2010.Resource detailing current laboratory methods & research techniques in the field of neuroscience. Focuses mostly on animal research - yet, I still recommend it as a resource for us human researchers as it supports our understanding of relevant animal research literature.
Clinical Practice
  • Campbell WW, Barohn RJ. DeJong's The Neurologic Examination. 8th ed. Wolters Kluwer; 2020.Clinical resource offering an in-depth understanding of neurologic exams. Includes detailed neurologic examination techniques with illustrations and guidelines for how to implement them, as well as an explanation of the underlying neuroanatomy and diagnoses. Covers different systems spanning sensory, motor, mental status.
Gait & Biomechanics

Resources for analysing kinematic and kinetic measures in walking or other movements.

  • Winter, D.A. Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement. 4th Edition, John Wiley & Sons, Hoboken.2009. In-depth resource to understand biomechanics of movement, including foundations & clinical applications, as well as how to collect & analyse biomechanics data. Particularly helpful to begin the process of analysing kinematic and kinetic measures in walking - it can serves a reference for expected data and practical resource for how to approach implementation & data analysis.
  • Brian Kwon. Biomechanical Foundation. Dr. Kwon's Golf Biomechanics WebsiteHelpful intuitive introduction to biomechanics
  • Neumann, Donald A. Kinesiology of the Musculoskeletal System.Second Edition, Mosby/Elsevier, 2010.Comprehensive principles of kinesiology spanning both upper and lower limb muscles and joints - with applications to movement rehabilitation & showing examples of typical and atypical patterns.
  • Saunders N. Whittlesey and D. Gordon E. Robertson. Two-Dimensional Inverse Dynamics.Chapter 5 of Research Methods in Biomechanics.Great resource with step-by-step guidelines for implementing inverse dynamics computations, useful for bridging kinetics and kinematics & estimating forces and moments using kinematic data when no direct force measurement is avaialble. E.g., it can be useful for investigating forces/moments at knee or other joints not directly in contact with force plates.
